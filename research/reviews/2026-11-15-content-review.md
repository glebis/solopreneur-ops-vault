## New Concepts

### 1. Semantic Content Fingerprinting

**The shift:** LinkedIn's algorithm now reads for meaning, not hashtags. Every post leaves a semantic fingerprint — a content signature that either reinforces or dilutes your topic authority graph. This is an evolution of Topic DNA, but it operates at a deeper level: not just "am I posting about Claude Code?" but "is the substance of this post recognizably about non-developer practitioners building AI tools?"

**Framework outline:**
- **Core claim:** Topic authority isn't built by hashtags or keywords — it's built by semantic consistency. A post about "productivity" that never mentions your practitioner lens doesn't reinforce the graph. A post about "AI tools" that always ties back to non-technical users does.
- **Practical implication:** Every post should contain at least one "semantic anchor" — a phrase, concrete example, or framing that is unambiguously within your topic cluster. The anchor doesn't have to be explicit (you don't need to say "this is about Claude Code"); it just has to be semantically consistent.
- **Diagnostic:** Read your last 10 posts and ask: could this post have been written by someone teaching a completely different AI topic? If yes, the semantic fingerprint is weak.
- **Content angle:** "The LinkedIn signal you're not optimizing: it's not hashtags, it's meaning" — Contrarian + How-To hybrid, high dwell potential.
- **Why now:** Confirmed in November 2026 as the primary routing mechanism. The "semantic fingerprint" framing is not yet in circulation; it's yours to name and own.

**File to create:** `frameworks/Semantic Content Fingerprinting.md`

### 2. Short-Form AI Demo Architecture

**The shift:** LinkedIn is actively pushing portrait-orientation video (15–90 seconds). AI educators who demo tools in landscape screencasts are missing the mobile-reach multiplier. But short-form AI demos require a different architecture than long-form: you can't set up context, run a prompt, and explain the output in 60 seconds. You need a structure that communicates the value in the format.

**Framework outline:**
- **The 4-part structure for 60-second AI demos:**
  1. **Hook (0–5s):** State the outcome, not the tool. "I turned a 60-minute meeting into a structured task list in 20 seconds." Not: "Here's how to use Claude Code."
  2. **Demo (5–45s):** Show the input → output transition, not the full workflow. Cut everything except the moment the value becomes visible.
  3. **Annotation (45–55s):** One caption or voiceover line that names the specific skill or technique visible in the demo. "The key: frontmatter schema so Claude reads structure, not prose."
  4. **CTA (55–60s):** One specific, low-friction invitation. "Which step in your workflow would you automate first? Drop it below."
- **Portrait mode imperative:** Record at 9:16 (portrait). LinkedIn gives native video a reach multiplier; portrait video gets the full mobile multiplier. Landscape video viewed on mobile loses 30%+ of the frame and signals "not built for this platform."
- **Captions are non-negotiable:** 2.3× engagement multiplier. Burn them in before uploading.
- **Content angle:** "The 60-second AI demo structure that gets watched on mobile" — directly applicable to Tuesday teaching posts.

**File to create:** `frameworks/Short-Form AI Demo Architecture.md`
