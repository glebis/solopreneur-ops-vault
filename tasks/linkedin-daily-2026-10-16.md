**Suggested comment:**
> "kepano's framing is exactly right, and the 'how do I structure it' question in this thread has a short answer that I've tested: flat folders, consistent frontmatter, and a MOC (map of content) file per topic area. Agents navigate by keyword search and by frontmatter properties — they don't 'browse' the way a human does. So the structure that works for an agent is the one that makes each note discoverable by two or three key terms and locatable by a known property (type, status, date). The vault structure that feels *tidy* to you (deep folder hierarchies, complex tagging systems) tends to be the one that confuses agents most. A vault designed for human navigation and a vault designed for agent access look almost opposite. The practical shift: put the metadata in the frontmatter, put the context in the first paragraph, and keep the folder depth at two levels maximum. That combination handles both the human and the agent."

The people whose AI-generated posts sound generic usually don't know what their own voice actually looks like in text — which is a separate and more interesting problem to solve."
